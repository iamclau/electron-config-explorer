    How to read this page

    Aufbau principle: electrons fill the lowest-energy subshell first (lower n + l first; on a tie, lower n first).
    Pauli exclusion: an orbital holds at most 2 electrons, with opposite spins (↑↓).
    Hund's rule: in p, d and f, each orbital gets one electron before any orbital gets a second.
    3D atom: each glowing dot is one electron, and its little cone points ↑ or ↓ for its spin. Shapes use the block colors from the table; the light and dark halves are the two phases. Orbitals with one electron are see-through, and full ones are solid.
    Orbital boxes: rows are energy levels (n). Columns are subshell types: s has 1 orbital, p has 3, d has 5, f has 7.
    Keys: ← → step, Space play, Home reset.

    Orbital shapes

    Each dot is a place the electron could be found, so dense regions are where it's most likely. Orange and blue show the wave's phase (+ or −). A node is a surface where the wave crosses zero, so the electron is never found there. Click any orbital box above to open it here.
